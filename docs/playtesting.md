## How the runs were played

A Playwright driver played each run with real inputs: mouse clicks on the in-scene boards, a mouse-drag yaw plus a held `W` to walk through the entry's first door, mouse drags from a fan card to an automaton (fling) or onto the table (drop), mouse-drawn sigils traced on the plate from the sigil's template strokes (every second trace jittered by 3 % of the plate), `T` to throw dice and `E` to end the turn. Debug helpers only reached states: `__combat.teleportToEncounter()` to stand at the next fight, and in runs marked `win`, `__flow.setCheat('win')` before elites and the guardian (they open at 1 hp), so a run reaches victory and a funded workshop. The bot blocks while the incoming intent damage exceeds its block and otherwise attacks. It is a test policy, so its deaths are not a balance verdict.

Each run went: title, New Run, a real door crossing, every fight in order with its reward, a reload to the title and Continue after the first reward, the guardian, death or victory, the summary board, title, the workshop (buying an upgrade when affordable), back to the title, and a second New Run in the same page to prove the loop restarts.

## Observations

### Fling (desktop drag)

- Targeted cards dragged at an automaton resolved through the point fallback (`via point`), and untargeted cards dropped on the table resolved as drops (`via drop`). Of 561 drags over the eight runs, 481 played or staged. The rest were the sigil soft-lock (F2) and dice gating ("Throw the dice first"), not misses.
- A drop needs the release over the table. Before F1 the table sat below the view, so a desktop player could only drop by dragging below the screen edge.

### Dice

- 36 of 36 throws settled with the physical up face equal to the rules roll (the up face index mapped through the die's `faces`), so the D1 seeded-face correction held on desktop. Whether the correction is visible needs the headset.
- Cards that roll gate everything else until `T` throws ("Throw the dice first"). That reads well in the HUD. The Throw button was out of the desktop view when this was written (O2, fixed in F3).

### Sigils (mouse)

- Ward traced cleanly gave 10 block (38 casts) and the jittered trace gave 8 block (33 casts), so the score-to-multiplier mapping shows in the numbers.
- Before F2, the second sigil card played within 1.6 s of a cast never got a plate (SPIRE002, room 4, Arc Bolt). The fight sat in the sigil stage refusing every card until End Turn.
- Only Ward was traced by the bot in these runs. The other four sigils are covered under Sigil traces (desktop) below.

### Comfort

Not observable on desktop. Seated comfort, snap turn, the vignette and teleport need the Quest 3 run.

## Sigil traces (desktop)

Method: headless Chromium on the GPU (Apple M4 Max, 1600x1000) against the dev server, `?seed=BASH7000&debug`, the guardian fight in room 5 (160 hp, so nothing dies mid-run). Before each cast the player's hand was set to the one sigil card and steam to 9 through the debug controller state, so the five cards could be cast back to back. Findings:

- All 15 casts were recognized as their own sigil. A smooth hand-like trace scores 0.88 to 0.93 (multiplier 1.04 to 1.19), a rough hand with independent per-point noise 0.80 to 0.89 (0.80 to 1.07), and a clean trace 0.96 to 1.00 (the 1.25 cap). No sigil is an outlier, so the templates, the resampling and the desktop plate projection needed no change. Ward is the most sensitive to a rough hand (0.801), Bolt the least (0.891).
- Flame and Spiral score 0.960 and 0.972 on a perfect trace because the plate's spacing filter drops their dense template points. Both stay above the 0.95 perfect threshold, so they still earn the full multiplier.
- The pure scorer in `src/sigil/**` was not touched.
- The traces exposed F4 (the dragged card springing back after a sigil cast). Before the fix, 5 of the 6 Bolt and Flame casts after the first needed a second drag (`dragAttempts` 2 in the run log). After it, all 15 casts took one drag.

## Automated full run (P5-T12 criterion 1, desktop)

`scripts/full-run/run.mjs` (`make full-run`) plays seeded runs of the real game on the dev server in headless Chromium on the GPU (`--use-angle=metal --enable-gpu --ignore-gpu-blocklist`, 1600x1000), one fresh browser context (own IndexedDB) per seed. Every verb is a player input: mouse clicks on the boards, a mouse-drag turn, WASD walking room to room through the doors along an A* path over the floor's footprints and colliders, fights opened by walking into an automaton's trigger radius, cards dragged at an automaton or onto the table, `T` to throw, sigils traced with the mouse on the plate from the card's template strokes, `E` to end the turn, then the reward pick, the summary board, the workshop (buys an upgrade when affordable), and a second New Run in the same page. The `?debug` hooks are only read (state, screen positions); no cheat and no teleport. The death run ends turns without playing a card.

The policy plays only from what a player sees (hand, steam, hp, block, statuses, the intent on each enemy). It never reads the draw pile, an rng stream or an enemy's later moves. Each turn it searches the orders in which the cards in hand can be played, with every legal target, and models damage, block, Weak, Vulnerable, Wound-up and Overheat with the mean of each die and a perfectly traced sigil (x1.25). It scores a turn's end as the damage dealt (a bonus per kill, Overheat counted over its decay, a bonus for Wound-up and for draws) minus 4 times the hp the visible intents would take through the block, with a large penalty for dying and a large bonus for a kill that ends the fight, then plays the first card of the best order and searches again, so real rolls and draws replace the means. It ends the turn when no play beats ending it. That puts block where an intent hits, damage where an enemy's block is down, and Overwind and the Vulnerable and Weak cards ahead of the attacks they strengthen. Rewards: the offered card with the highest fixed value for the deck (Arc Bolt, Furnace Brand, Escapement Hammer, Warding Glyph first, repeats discounted except Furnace Brand, whose Overheat stacks), or a skip below the floor value. Map: the floor has no rest, healing or upgrade room (a rest room only carries its role), so the choice is which fights to take and in what order. The bot clears every non-elite fight in the side wings first (door order, nearest first), then walks the route to the guardian and, just before the guardian, opens every treasure coffer whose way from the entry holds no uncleared encounter (see Treasure coffers below). That order is deliberate: a pickup draws from the `loot` stream the rewards share, so opening one earlier would change every later offer and the deck the elite and the guardian meet (a first run that opened coffers right after the side fights lost SPIRE034's win). An elite is fought only when it stands on that route, so a floor whose elite sits in a side wing never meets it. This is a test policy, so its deaths are not a balance verdict.

## Frame instrumentation: the FPS counter and `?perf` windows

Two tools find slowdowns in the headset. The FPS counter is a small gauge (fps and the longest recent frame against the 72 Hz budget) that floats in front of the view on the desktop and starts above the left wrist in XR. Point either controller at the gauge, hold its grip button, move the gauge, and release. The gauge stays in that position relative to the player and faces the viewer for the rest of the XR session. A settings row switches it on and off at runtime (`perf.setFpsCounter(on)`), with no URL flag. The frame recorder runs while the counter is on, or from boot with `?perf` (any value but `0`; `?perf=1` also mounts the debug panel). Off, a frame costs a null check.

The recorder (`src/perf/recorder.ts`) keeps every frame interval in a 131,072-entry ring and cuts the session into windows. A mark closes the open window and opens the next. Each window drops its first second (the settle after a mark, so a load hitch or a camera cut does not count), then closes into a summary: `frames`, `meanMs`, `p50Ms`, `p95Ms`, `p99Ms` (nearest rank), `maxMs` (the longest frame), `fps`, `overBudgetPct` (frames slower than 1.5x the budget, a missed vsync) and `over2xBudgetPct` (slower than 2.5x, two missed vsyncs), plus `targetHz`, `budgetMs`, `foveation`, `scale` and `presenting`. A window closed inside its settle, or empty, is not kept. A frame gap over 5 s (system menu, hidden tab) is not counted as a frame. Summaries persist to `localStorage` key `rogue-xr.perf.v1` on each close (at most 200 windows and 400,000 characters, oldest dropped first). Desktop windows have no `targetHz`, so no budget shares.

Slow frames are also logged one by one as hitches (`__spireDiag().perf.hitches`, stored beside the windows, newest 500 kept): every frame longer than 1.5x the budget (1.5x 60 Hz on the desktop), with the open window's `label`, its `ms`, `sinceMarkMs` and `presenting`. Hitches include the frames a window drops in its settle and the first frame after a mark, which is where the work a mark labels lands, so a `dice-throw` or `cast` hitch shows here even when its window is too short to keep.

A summary's `label` is why its window opened and `closedBy` is why it ended. The game marks these automatically:

| Label | Opens when |
| --- | --- |
| `boot` | the first window |
| `xr-start`, `xr-end` | the XR session starts or ends |
| `room <index> <template>` | the player enters a room |
| `phase combat` | a fight starts |
| `phase explore` | back to exploring (a fight ended, or fled) |
| `phase reward` | the reward board opens |
| `chest-opened`, `chest-claimed` | a coffer opens, or its card is claimed |
| `cast` | a sigil cast starts, until `play` 3 s later (a short window around the cast and its FX) |
| `dice-throw`, `dice-settled` | a die is thrown, and when it settles (the `dice-throw` window ends at the settle) |
| `foveation <level>`, `scale <factor>` | foveation or framebuffer scale changes |
| `pagehide` | the page unloads, so the open window persists |
| `recorder-off` | the counter went off with no `?perf` |
| anything else | `__spirePerfMark('label')` from the console or a CDP call |

Reading the windows from the Quest: play with the counter on (or open `https://spire.pardev.net/?perf`), then connect over USB with developer mode on and `adb devices` showing the headset. Open `chrome://inspect#devices` in desktop Chrome, inspect the Quest Browser tab, and in its console run `__spireDiag().perf.windows` once (a single read; polling it every frame would itself cost frame time). `__spireDiag().perf.current` is the open window as if it closed now, and `__spirePerfMark('before-fight')` cuts a window by hand. With the counter off and `?perf` absent the same call still returns the windows stored by an earlier session. `__spirePerfClear()` empties them. Without DevTools, the same JSON is under the `rogue-xr.perf.v1` key in the page's `localStorage`.

Reading hitches: a hitch with `sinceMarkMs` under one frame belongs to the marked moment itself (the throw, the cast, entering the room). Reading a window: compare `p95Ms` and `maxMs` against `budgetMs` (13.9 ms at 72 Hz). `over2xBudgetPct` above a few percent in a `room`, `cast` or `dice-throw` window names the moment to look at. Compare windows only at the same `foveation` and `scale`. Desktop frame numbers only rank variants. Only a headset window closes a budget criterion.

For combat profiling, use `?perf=quest`. Read `__spireDiag().perf.quest` once after the run. Each fight has separate XR and desktop callback counts, FPS, approximate p95, longest interval, and intervals over the 72 Hz or 60 Hz budget. Gaps over 250 ms are listed separately. The fight stays open through markers for throws and casts. An interrupted fight is retained when the page exits.

The capture also records timed CPU stages of at least 0.5 ms, with per-fight counts and maxima and timestamps on recent samples. Compare `combat.dice.throw-lookahead`, `combat.dice`, `combat.ui`, `combat.effects`, `floor.update`, and `render.submit.cpu` against slow periods. Render submission measures CPU time. Use VrApi for device compositor and GPU evidence.
