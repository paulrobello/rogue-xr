# Clockwork Spire puzzle recipe book

Build an encounter by choosing a clue, a place to find it, something to manipulate, and a visible consequence. A found object can be an answer, a lead to the answer, or a part the player needs to carry back. The rows below are ingredients for different rooms and floors. They do not all belong in Pressure Works.

## Pressure Works

A loose maintenance dispatch directs the player toward one of the missing service slips. Three slips in different rooms record one reading apiece for circuits A, B, and C. A slip can lie on furniture or the floor, turn up inside a coffer, or drop after a fight. Picking it up opens it for reading and files it in the run’s **Items** page. An opened chest or cleared room keeps its uncollected slip available on return.

Set the three valves, then operate the primer. The machinery stirs, vents steam, and releases the catch on the maker’s mark. A scratched hint points to the mark. Pull it to open the built-in drawer, then take the cogs. This separates solving, discovering the compartment, and claiming the reward. The gallery no longer prints the combination.

## Ingredients for future puzzles

| Ingredient | What the player can do or notice | A possible payoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Loose or hidden paper | Lift a folded letter from a desk, a bench, or beneath a chair. Read a direct value, a drawing, or directions to another room. | The paper remains available in Items. A lead can point to a particular coffer, enemy, or inscription. |
| Inscription | Inspect a value scratched into a wall, a floor tile or the underside of furniture. Lamp light can make the groove visible from one angle. | Copy the mark into Items or use it to identify which fitting to pull. |
| Book or wall fitting | Pull one worn book, twist an unlit sconce, or tug the brass boss that shows handling marks. | A shelf swings away as a secret door, or a smaller compartment slides out. Use a different wear pattern on the moving piece. |
| Directional painting | Follow the sitter’s gaze, a painted hand, a line of gears, or a depicted door missing from the real wall. | Find a concealed latch elsewhere in the room. The painting can stay on its hook. |
| Painting that changes on return | Leave the room and come back. A painted figure has moved, a key is gone, or a different number of moons hangs above a tower. | The difference identifies a real object or a hiding place. Tie the change to a room-entry or story event and save it so a reload cannot erase the clue. |
| Wall clock | Hear a distinctive chime and see a shutter, beam of light, or moving hand reveal something elsewhere in the same room at a particular time. | Pick up a briefly exposed object or record an inscription. A winding key or settable hands can let players recreate the moment. |
| Item receptacles | Gather objects across rooms and place them into matching sockets, bowls, or niches. Shapes, engravings, and fit communicate which object belongs where. | Correct placement drives an interlock. Let players retrieve a misplaced object. |
| Ordered placement | Insert the same set of pieces in a particular sequence. A mural, maintenance log, or clock chime gives the order. | Each correct step moves a physical part or lights a register. Allow a reversible reset. |
| Gear train | Collect different-sized gears. Place them on spindles; the radii and gaps determine which teeth mesh and where the train breaks. | Turn the driver and see exactly where motion stops. A complete train can lift a bolt or rotate an orrery. |
| Wind-up key | Find a key with a matching arbor, insert it, and wind a spring. | Stored motion runs a rebuilt gear train, advances a clock, opens a panel, or changes a painting before the next visit. |
| Door and lighting variants | Use single leaves, paired double doors, an arched opening, or doors disguised as wall panels. Place tabletop, wall-mounted, and freestanding candelabras as landmarks. | Distinct silhouettes help the player remember the way back. Visible flames should move like the existing fire, with a gentler candle-scale motion and reduced-motion support. |

## Example combinations

- **The key in the light.** A stained-glass window casts colored shapes across the floor. Three teeth and a bow resolve into a key when seen together. Its tip points to a loose brass tile. Pressing the tile opens a small cache. Make the silhouette readable by brightness as well as color.
- **The absent door.** A painting of an arched double door points to an ordinary wall. After a return visit one painted leaf is open. A scratch in the corresponding real panel identifies the book that releases it.
- **The stalled clock.** A coffer holds a wind-up key. An enemy carries the missing gear. The player seats the gear between two differently sized wheels, winds the clock and watches its light reveal a floor inscription at the marked hour.
- **The curator’s inventory.** A dispatch names three objects without naming their sockets. A second room’s painting shows their order. Placing them makes successive parts of a cabinet move until its reward compartment opens.

## Room and encounter palette

- A room theme chooses from a kit of furnishing groups and verified sockets. A dining hall can combine a table, chairs, curtains, and a branched candelabrum. A library can use tall shelves, a reading chair, a clock, or a painting. Vary the combination, orientation, and accent finish from the floor seed; keep doors, interaction points, and the walking lane clear.
- A shelf may be plain, contain a loose clue, or slide away after a particular book is pulled. A single doorway opening can accept a plain leaf, a pair of leaves, or an arched surround. Choose the architecture separately from its lock and its contents.
- Standing armor can be dressing or a dormant clockwork opponent. A displaced visor, a moving gaze, or a faint ticking sound can foreshadow the encounter. Decide its role from the seed and persist an awakening, so leaving and returning cannot create another enemy.
- A time-based reveal can change a different object in the same room: a clock strikes, a shutter opens, and light falls on the underside of a chair. Paintings can also have distinct visit states independent of the clock. Preserve the first and later images for comparison in Items when that comparison is the puzzle.

## Twenty more recipes

These are design ingredients. The component kits supply physical starting pieces; a recipe becomes playable when its interactions, clues, persistence, and outcome are wired into a floor.

| No. | Recipe | Player action and visible result |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 1 | Shadow stencil | Slide a pierced shutter across a lantern. When its shadow sits inside a carved frame, the projected pattern identifies a floor tile. |
| 2 | Borrowed light | Tilt a brass mirror to send light around a blind corner. A distant receiver opens where the beam lands. |
| 3 | Cancelled inscription | Rotate a polarizing lens over two superimposed engravings. One layer fades until a legible shelf mark remains. |
| 4 | Prism signal | Turn a prism to split a source across three receivers. A notch, a star, and a ring distinguish the channels as well as their colors. |
| 5 | Misrouted post | Set a pneumatic selector and send a brass capsule. Retrieve its reply at the named station, or follow its rattle to find a crossed connection. |
| 6 | Speaking pipes | Sound one pipe and listen at the outlets. A vibrating gauge identifies the same outlet visually. Follow the matching line behind the wall. |
| 7 | Float elevator | Admit water to a sight tube. A brass float raises a sealed item to one of several access hatches; stop it at the reachable one. |
| 8 | Steam leak | Find the hissing joint, seat a recovered gasket, and repressurize the line. The repaired branch powers the next device. |
| 9 | Counterfeit counterweight | Two weights have the same crest but different mass. Compare them on a balance, then install the genuine one to lift a grille. |
| 10 | Pendulum rendezvous | Adjust the effective length of one pendulum until two pointers cross together. A mechanical catch engages on the shared beat. |
| 11 | Spring tension window | Wind a key until a gauge enters an engraved interval, then release the clutch. Too little tension only lifts the latch partway. |
| 12 | Ratchet handoff | Alternate two ratchets so one pawl holds while the other advances a rack. Both controls can be worked with one seated hand. |
| 13 | Mirror-type press | Turn a plate face-down in a press and stamp a scrap of paper. A previously reversed message becomes a readable inventory note. |
| 14 | Missing drum pin | A pinned cylinder plays a repeating pattern with a gap. Move a spare pin into the silent position and wind it again. |
| 15 | Key behind the grille | Move a magnet across the outside of a grille to drag a loose key around steel obstructions to a retrieval slot. |
| 16 | Dark circuit | Lay conductor bridges so two lamps glow while the third stays dark. Raised symbols show the intended state without relying on color. |
| 17 | Wax rubbing | Lay paper over a worn relief plate and rub it. The new inventory item preserves a crest too shallow to read in room light. |
| 18 | Model and building | Move a tiny gate on a tabletop model of the Spire. A matching full-sized gate moves one room away, visible through a sightline. |
| 19 | Wrong-floor delivery | Load a dumbwaiter, select the landing, then follow a different passage to meet it. A marked cargo seal identifies the correct basket. |
| 20 | Breath on glass | Open a steam vent beneath a cold mirror. Condensation reveals a traced direction, then fades; the player can recreate and record it. |


## Asset shelf

The furnishings and instruments are node libraries. A room places selected roots and reuses their geometry and materials. Dining and library furniture, gallery paintings, clocks, armor as dressing, and lit candelabras can be selected by the runtime. A new floor can have one secret bookcase and one ordinary cabinet. Many floors have no secret. In a puzzle room, the clockmaker first appears in his original pose. After the player has seen him and left the room, the portrait changes when the entire frame is out of view in both eyes. Other paintings stay the same. [Curtains and gallery frames in Quest Browser](verification/clockwork-interiors-quest.jpg) The clockmaker looks toward a red book in the same room. Pull the red book with a nearby grip, a trigger ray, or a desktop click to receive the Clockmaker’s key in Items. Use the key on the lock below the shelves. Grab the exposed cogs or point and select them to collect the pile. The cogs fly toward the player and add 10 to the purse. [Gallery and cabinet in Quest Browser](verification/quest-gallery-corrections.jpg) Key, cabinet, and claimed-cog progress survives Continue and save import, and legacy saves whose `bookSecretOpenedRooms` already contains a room remain paid. Both the built-in study shelves and the freestanding case use individually drawn bindings and paper edges. [Study books in Quest Browser](verification/quest-study-book-bindings.jpg) Waking armor and walk-through secret passages remain future encounters.
